How to turn typed text into your own handwriting in GoodNotes

Updated November 2, 2026

In short

GoodNotes can’t turn typed text into handwriting by itself; it only goes the other way. Siebwrite can: write each character once, type or paste your text, and open the finished file in GoodNotes. It shows up in your handwriting as real strokes you can erase. Free, no account.

Can’t GoodNotes do this itself?

No, not as of November 2026. GoodNotes converts handwriting into typed text and can tidy messy handwriting with Restyle. The opposite direction, typed text into your handwriting, isn’t built in.

Typed text in GoodNotes is always a text box in a font. It looks like a keyboard, every e is identical, and the eraser can’t take out single letters.

How to do it with Siebwrite

  1. Write your alphabet. Open the Siebwrite app in your browser, ideally on an iPad with Apple Pencil. Write each of the 80 characters once in its box. It takes about five minutes. No time? Start with one of the five ready-made handwritings.
  2. Paste your text. Switch to “Compose” and type your text, or paste it from Word, a website, your notes or a chatbot. Long texts are spread across several pages automatically.
  3. Set up the page. Pick the paper (wide or narrow ruled, squared or blank), the format (A3, A4, A5 or GoodNotes), color and spacing. Don’t like how a spot looks? Tap “Shuffle” and the letters come out a little different.
  4. Open it in GoodNotes. Tap “Import into GoodNotes”. On iPad and iPhone the share sheet opens; pick GoodNotes. On a computer the .goodnotes file downloads. Each page becomes its own file.

From then on it’s handwriting like any other: erase single words, move lines with the lasso, keep writing with your pen.

What about Apple Notes and Smart Script?

Since iPadOS 18, the Notes app on iPad can paste copied text in a handwriting style similar to yours (Apple’s guide). Handy, but for GoodNotes it has three catches:

  • It only works inside Apple Notes. The text doesn’t come over to GoodNotes as handwriting.
  • It only runs on supported iPads, and Notes first needs saved handwriting of yours with at least ten different lowercase letters.
  • Apple recreates your style with a model. With Siebwrite, every letter comes from your own stroke.

If you only write in Apple Notes, that’s a good option. If you study in GoodNotes, you need another way.

Frequently asked questions

Does Siebwrite cost anything?

No. No subscription, no ads, no watermark and no limit on how many pages you export. You don’t need an account either.

Won’t it look computer-made?

Every letter comes from your own stroke and is drawn slightly differently each time it appears: size, slant and spacing vary a little. If you add variants later, they take turns too, and the handwriting looks even more natural.

Can I edit the text afterwards?

In GoodNotes you erase single words or letters and rewrite them with your pen. For bigger changes, edit the text in Siebwrite and import the page again.

Does it work without an iPad?

Yes. Siebwrite runs in any browser, including Android, Windows and Mac. You write the alphabet with a stylus, finger or mouse. GoodNotes is only needed for the .goodnotes file; everything else works as a PDF.

Does it work in Notability or OneNote?

The .goodnotes file is for GoodNotes only. For other apps, use the PDF. There the writing is part of the PDF page and can’t be erased stroke by stroke.

Where is my handwriting stored?

Only in your browser; Siebwrite has no server. If you choose to sign in with Google, a copy also goes into your own Google Drive so it’s there on your other devices.
